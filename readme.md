# [Leitong Integrated] Radar and Communication Dual-Function Beamforming Design Based on RIS [with MATLAB Code]

1. Introduction:
Since the widespread use of radar systems in the last century, their development has been hindered by the issue of spectrum scarcity, which is a problem that is exacerbated by interference from next-generation wireless communication systems. Dual-function radar and communication (DFRC) systems are considered to be a promising solution for this issue as they integrate radar and communication systems on the same hardware platform and operate simultaneously. However, the performance achievable region is limited by the capabilities of base stations (BS). Therefore, in this project, we propose the deployment of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) DFRC systems to expand the achievable region. RIS can intelligently control the propagation environment of electromagnetic waves by adjusting the amplitude and phase of its reflective elements.
Specifically, in this project, we studied the optimization of passive beamforming in a dual-function BS with active beamforming and separate or shared deployments based on an optimized joint optimization of RIS and active beamforming. To maximize the weighted sum rate (WSR) at the communication user and the detection power at the target simultaneously, two methods based on the weighted minimum mean square error (WMMSE) framework and fractional programming (FP) were proposed for alternating optimization (AO), respectively for separate and shared deployments. Unlike many literature considering a single connected RIS model, this project developed a generalized group or fully connected RIS model. Simulation results show that RIS can help achieve better transmission beamforming, improve the upper bound of WSR, and expand the achievable region.
2. RIS-assisted DFRC system:
RIS-assisted DFRC system remains an open research area with limited work [30, 31, 32]. Wang et al. [30] first investigated the RIS-assisted radar-communication coexistence where maximizing the detection probability of the radar is achieved by optimizing both active and passive beamforming while ensuring the received SINR at the communication users. The problem of maximizing detection probability can be transformed into minimizing the interference from the radar echoes. In [31], the DFRC system was considered initially. Similarly, in the context of user's SINR constraints, maximizing the echo SINR was considered. A two-stage algorithm was designed for the non-convex joint beamforming optimization and SIR improvement in radar and communication. In [32], the RIS-assisted DFRC system was also studied. Unlike the previous two works, the purpose of beamforming was to form a pre-designed beam pattern and eliminate multi-user interference (MUI). Trade-off between radar and communication performance was also studied. However, as mentioned earlier, in the context of DFRC and RIS-assisted systems, WSR is more representative than SINR and MUI. Therefore, in this project, the goal is to maximize the WSR in the user's RIS dead DFRC system.
In this project, we aim to design a RIS-assisted multi-antenna DFRC system and jointly design the active and passive beamforming in a dual-function BS and RIS, to maximize the WSR in both users and target detection power simultaneously.
As mentioned earlier, there are only a few works in the RIS-assisted DFRC field [30, 31, 32], and we explore different communication and radar metrics (i.e., WSR and detection power). Based on the qualitative analysis in Section 1.1, RIS can achieve larger reachable areas for DFRC systems. The following sections will present quantitative results. Two deployment scenarios of dual-function BSs, separated and shared deployments [9, 23] were considered. In these two deployments, the joint active and passive beamforming designs were converted into optimization problems and solved alternatively. The generalized RIS model [2] was compared with the traditional cascading RIS models in terms of WSR enhancement in both Rayleigh and LOS channels.
In this project, we proposed an active and passive beamforming design for RIS-assisted DFRC that allows communication with downlink users and detection targets in a fascinating direction in both separated and shared deployments. The main contributions of this project are listed below.
3. Main Contributions of the Project:
1. We proposed a joint beamforming design between the BS and RIS to maximize WSR and detection power in target separation and shared deployments. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study on maximizing WSR in a RIS-assisted DFRC system. As in [9], we also consider the detection power as a radar metric for clear trade-off comparison. However, the logarithmic form of WSR and additional radar signal terms, detection power quadratic form, and quadratic equality power constraints make the optimization of the joint beamforming nonconvex and difficult to achieve.
2. This is also the first work to investigate new group or fully connected RIS models in RIS-assisted DFRC. This new RIS model can improve SNR performance, especially in Rayleigh fading channels [2], but in this project, we studied its potential benefits in expanding the WSR and detection power achievable regions in DFRC.
3. We proposed an AO algorithm, suggesting the use of separate deployment non-convex designs. As mentioned in [9], the active beamforming problem is rephrased as a semidefinite programming (SDP) problem using the WMMSE framework. Based on Lagrangian dual transformation [25], quadratic transformation [24] and scattering-impedance relationship [2], the passive beamforming problem is rephrased as an unconstrained problem. Compared with the passive beamforming optimization method in [6], this method is an extended form that considers an additional radar signal term and generalized RIS model.
4. We also proposed another AO algorithm to solve for shared deployment non-convex designs. The optimal active beamforming can be obtained through the WMMSE framework and semidefinite relaxation (SDR) [33]. Compared with the optimization minimization (MM) method that requires several steps to reach the optimal value used in [9], the SDR method only requires one step, thus having lower complexity. The same method is used to optimize the passive beamforming.
